## 📖 What Is Spatial Transcriptomics?

Cells in your body do not work alone. Their behavior depends heavily on where they sit inside a tissue. Traditional single-cell analysis breaks tissue apart and reads each cell in isolation, which loses that positional information entirely. Spatial transcriptomics keeps the location. It measures which genes are active in each cell while preserving the physical map of the tissue.

For a non-technical reader, the simplest way to think about it is a map of a city. Single-cell data tells you what kinds of buildings exist. Spatial data tells you what kinds of buildings exist *and* which street each one is on. That extra dimension changes the conclusions you can draw, especially in cancer research, developmental biology, neuroscience, and immunology.

Because the field combines imaging, sequencing, and heavy computation, the software landscape is fragmented. That fragmentation is exactly what this repository addresses.

## 🔄 The Order of an Analysis

The defining feature of this repository is its ordering. Rather than an alphabetical dump, entries are arranged the way a project actually unfolds:

1. **Acquire and load data.** Get your spatial dataset into a usable format.
2. **Quality control.** Remove low-quality spots and cells before they distort your results.
3. **Normalize.** Put samples on a comparable scale so differences reflect biology rather than technique.
4. **Cluster and annotate.** Group similar regions and assign them biological identities.
5. **Map spatially.** Overlay results onto the tissue image to see patterns in place.
6. **Detect spatial structure.** Find regions, gradients, and cell neighborhoods that matter.
7. **Integrate modalities.** Combine gene expression with imaging or histology.
8. **Interpret and visualize.** Turn results into figures you can present and publish.

Following this order prevents the most common beginner mistake: jumping straight to a flashy visualization before the data has been cleaned.
